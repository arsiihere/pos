## 2. Findings

### MAJOR

**M1. MAJOR — §3.5, §5.2 — `closes_on: { after_ms }` never fires, so `test_alert` never expires.**
*Problem:* The tick only looks at `alerts WHERE next_due_at <= now` (§3.5 pre-check and `due` select). §5.2 sets `next_due_at` to "time of the next undue step", and to null when no step is pending (§3.2, §5.1 comment). The `after_ms` check sits inside the loop over `due` (§3.5 pseudo-code: "if closes_on after_ms reached").
*Failure:* (1) A manager sends `SendTestAlert` → step 0 is written at raise, and `test_alert` has no step 1 → `next_due_at = null`. (2) At 600 s the alert is never selected, so it stays open until day close. (3) The receipt view and `alerts_open` keep showing it, and the tablet re-shows it on every reconnect. Any 03a / 01e kind that relies on `after_ms` after its last step (for example a device-offline alert with an auto-close) has the same bug. No test covers it: tests 17 and 46 never wait 600 s.
*Fix:* `next_due_at = min(next undue step time, raised_at + closes_on.after_ms)`. The tick tells the two apart (`expire` vs step). Add a test: `test_alert` closes `expired` at 600 s by the tick, and the pre-check is true at that moment.

**M2. MAJOR — §3.5 approval tier step 2, §3.3 rule 7, §6.9, §6 batch 3 — escalation drops the in-range approvers, and their cards never close.**
*Problem:* Step 2 `to` = `[{ branch_scope, answerer }, { holders_informed, informed }]`. A `my_team` lead is in range, so they are not in `holders_informed`. They are not branch-scope either, so step 2 drops them. Step 1's `always_on` devices are dropped too. That contradicts A6 / G1-9 ("escalation widens who is told, never who may answer"). The spec then relies on "latest step's recipients" in three places: `requests-to-answer` (§6.9), `alert_closed` "to every recipient" (§3.3 rule 7 and the post-commit read of `alert_recipients`, which is "replaced per step", §5.1), and `alerts_open` (§3.7).
*Failure:* (1) Hamza raises a void. Step 0 and step 1 go to his lead Imran (`my_team`) and the kiosk. (2) At 120 s, step 2 replaces the recipients with the manager plus the informed holders, and Imran's inbox gets `moved_to`. (3) Imran opens "Requests to answer": the request is gone, although he still may answer it. (4) The manager approves. `alert_closed` goes only to step 2's recipients. Imran's tablet and the kiosk never get it. Imran's client keeps the "one reminder every 2 min until closed" (§8.3) going until he taps it and gets `409`. The kiosk badge stays up.
*Fix:* Step 2 = `[{ answerers, answerer }, { holders_informed, informed }, { always_on, recipient }]`, which widens. Send `alert_closed` (and compute `alerts_open` and the receipt "was sent" check, m3) over the **cumulative** recipients: every person in `alert_inbox`, plus every device ever in a step. That needs a cumulative device table, or `alert_recipients` kept per step instead of replaced. `requests-to-answer` uses `alert_inbox` plus `can_answer` computed at read time. Tests: a `my_team` answerer still sees the request after the escalation, and every earlier recipient gets `alert_closed`.

**M3. MAJOR — §3.3 rules 2, 4, 5 — in-person self-approval with a known PIN is undetectable, and the PIN check is a silent oracle.**
*Problem (a):* PINs get shared in restaurants. With `method: 'in_person'`, a waiter can name any approver and type that approver's PIN on the waiter's own tablet. Rule 5 only blocks naming yourself. The record says "Imran approved in person on Hamza's tablet", and Imran is never told.
*Problem (b):* Rule 4 runs `checkPin` **before** rule 1. A correct PIN on any `alert_id`, including a random ULID, reaches `onMatch`, which throws `404 ALERT_NOT_FOUND` and rolls everything back. `resetStaffCounter` then runs again in its own transaction (`pin.ts:494`). So a correct guess leaves **no event at all**, while a sign-in would leave `SessionStarted`. Wrong guesses count against the manager (5 failures → `StaffPinLockedOut`, `schema.ts:6`) and the device (10 per 5 min, `schema.ts:7–8`).
*Failure:* (1) Hamza sends `AnswerRequest { alert_id: <random>, method: 'in_person', answerer_staff_id: <manager>, pin: 1234 }` and gets `401`. He retries with another PIN and gets `404` = PIN confirmed, with nothing recorded. (2) Later he raises a void, approves it "in person" as the manager, and 04 removes the item. (3) Only a report that nobody has specified would show `method: 'in_person'`.
*Fix:* (1) Run rules 1, 3 and 5 against a read-only pre-read **before** `checkPin`: the alert exists, has a request part and is pending (`409` before argon2 is fine), and the answerer is not the requester. Keep the authoritative re-check inside the transaction. Probes then need a real pending request. (2) After every `in_person` answer, raise an `info` alert to the named answerer (`person` target, all routes): "You approved Void · T12 on Hamza's tablet". (3) §4.3 To 04 / 05 / 07: in-person answers are listed in the void and discount reports. Tests: a probe on an unknown alert runs no argon2, and the notice reaches the named answerer.

**M4. MAJOR — §6.2, §8.1, C-00 (missing row) — the approver's PIN is stored where it can be recovered.**
*Problem:* `AnswerRequest` is the first command with a PIN in its payload. The pipeline stores `payloadHash(command.payload)` = `sha256(canonicalJson(payload))` in `processed_commands` (`route.ts:147`, `hash.ts:5–7`). Every other field is known (`alert_id`, `decision`, `method`, `answerer_staff_id`), so 10⁴–10⁸ candidate PINs reverse it in seconds. The table lives in the hub database, in its nightly USB backups and in the pre-migration copies (foundation §5.8 step 1a). Also, the Android client has a local SQLite command queue and the cashier an IndexedDB queue (foundation §1, D56). Nothing in §8.1 says `AnswerRequest` is never queued, so a queued answer keeps the PIN in plain text on a shared tablet.
*Failure:* Someone with a backup file, or a tablet with a queued answer, recovers every manager PIN that was ever used for an approval.
*Fix:* (1) C-00 row: the pipeline hashes the payload **without** the fields that `pinCheck` returns (M19 replay still matches on `command_id`, device and type). (2) §8.1: `AnswerRequest` with a PIN is online-only, never queued; the PIN is held in memory only until the response arrives. (3) §7 Logging already bans PINs; add `processed_commands` and the client queue to that rule. Test: no stored hash or row can be matched against the PIN (the hash equals the hash of the payload without `pin`).

**M5. MAJOR — §3.12, §5.4 `AlertDisplaySchema`, §6 batch 3 — the approver cannot see what they are approving.**
*Problem:* `display` = `{ table_code?, point_code?, requester_name?, kind_label_key }`. The `card` (signed-in full text) has no shape and no fields for item, quantity, amount or reason. §4.3 To 05 says amounts and reasons "live in the owning module's events", but nothing carries them to the approver's card.
*Failure:* The manager's card reads "Void request · T12 · Hamza". Hamza voids a Rs 4 800 platter. The manager taps Approve + PIN thinking it is a drink. This hollows out the approval control (E-REQ), and 04 / 05 cannot add the fields without a 01d bump.
*Fix:* `defineAlertKind({ …, card_schema: z.object(…) })`. `raise` takes `display.card` validated by that schema. It is sent only on person-recipient staff sockets (§6 batch 3 rule unchanged) and never logged (§7). `lock` stays a separate, fixed subset. Test: a `t_approval` card with an amount reaches a staff socket and never an alert-mode or device-target socket.

**M6. MAJOR — §3.5 step `to`, §3.4 — later kinds' steps cannot be expressed without a 01d bump.**
*Problem:* A step target is `raised_target` or one of `answerers`, `branch_scope`, `holders_informed`, `always_on`, `managers`. The kinds §4.3 hands to later modules need more: 03a Ready to serve "last-attempt escalation to the **point** and managers" (the raised target is the table's team, so the point is not reachable). 02 handover "2 min → **sender's lead**" (no requester-lead target). The slip warning goes to the table's order taker. `zone_order_taker` is named in `registerTargetResolver` (§4.2) but missing from the §3.4 table.
*Failure:* 03a writes its kind and finds no way to say "step 2 → point `values.point_id`", so it needs a 01d bump before 03a can build.
*Fix:* Let a step entry name a target built from the alert: `{ target: { kind: 'point', from_value: 'point_id' }, set }`, `{ target: { kind: 'team', lead: 'requester_lead', depth: 'direct' }, set }`, `{ target: { kind: 'table_team', from_value: 'table_id' }, set }`. Or allow `to: (alert) => Array<{ target: AlertTarget, set }>`, pure. Add `zone_order_taker` to §3.4. Test with a fake 03a-like kind.

**M7. MAJOR — §3.5 request part, §4.2 — the `apply` / `onWithdraw` contract is untyped.**
*Problem:* `apply(ctx, answer): EventDraft[]` and `onWithdraw?(ctx)`. Neither `ctx` nor `answer` is defined. The alert record (subject, `values`, requester, `receiver_staff_id`) is not passed. For a handler-checked command, `ctx.permission` is `null` (H-3). 04's void `apply` needs the order and line, and 02's handover `apply` needs the table and the receiver.
*Failure:* Each owning module re-reads the `alerts` row itself, or puts ids into `dedupe_key` and parses them back out. Then the fix lands as a 01d bump in the middle of 04.
*Fix:* `apply(ctx: CommandCtx & { alert: AlertRecord }, answer: { decision, answerer_staff_id, method, device_id, answered_at }) → EventDraft[]`, and `onWithdraw(ctx & { alert })`. `AlertRecord` = the §3.2 fields with `values` typed by the kind (a `values_schema` on `defineAlertKind`).

**M8. MAJOR — §3.3 "No expiry", Q8, §3.5 day close — requests that no one can ever answer stay open forever, and nobody is told to close them.**
*Problem:* Requests never expire and survive day close. Q8 says owning modules close stale requests `obsolete`, but nothing tells them when one is stale. `resolve(requester, …)` on a requester removed from the directory gives `not_approver` for every answerer. 05's day close has no way to list open requests (01d exposes only `byDedupeKey`). §8.3 keeps "one reminder every 2 min **until closed**".
*Failure:* (1) At 23:40 Hamza raises a void, and the manager has gone home. (2) The day closes. Bindings clear, the request stays open, and the Z report is printed with a pending void that 05 cannot see. (3) The next morning the manager's tablet chimes every 2 min for yesterday's void. If Hamza was removed overnight, every answer returns `403 not_approver` and nothing closes the request.
*Fix:* (1) `defineAlertKind` request part gains `on_day_close: 'keep' | 'close_obsolete'` (04 and 05 choose). The tick applies it from the existing `expire_due` path. (2) Export `alerts.openRequests(db, { kind? })`, 1 statement, for 05's day-close check. (3) The `no_approver` dirty flag (already set on `StaffDirectoryApplied`) also makes the tick close `obsolete` (note "requester removed") any request whose requester is no longer in the directory. (4) The client stops the 2-min reminder for requests raised before the current business day. Tests for each.

**M9. MAJOR — §3.7 binding, §3.4.3 — a stale tablet binding swallows a person's alerts all day.**
*Problem:* The binding is kept on `idle_timeout` until someone else signs in on that tablet or the day closes (G1-13b). §3.4.3 counts a binding as a route, so the "no route → lead" fallback (A10, G1-18) never fires for that person. Receipts from that tablet are recorded as `staff_id = Ali` (§6 batch 3).
*Failure:* (1) Ali signs in on tablet T at 12:00 and idles out. His shift ends at 16:00, and he leaves T on the shelf and goes home. (2) At 19:00 the table-team target resolves to Ali. His "route" is T, so the alert goes there, the lead is never told, and the receipt view says "delivered to Ali". (3) Or Bilal picks up T without signing in and hears Ali's urgent repeats. Requests escalate (step 2), but non-request kinds (03a pickups to the table's team) depend on the fallback.
*Fix:* A binding counts as a route for §3.4.3 only while the person has had a session on any device within `ALERT_CONSTANTS.binding_route_ttl_ms` (e.g. 2 h), or is not marked absent. `PresenceSet { present: false }` ends that person's tablet bindings. `AlertDelivered.route = 'alert_connection'` is shown in the receipt view as "bound tablet, not signed in". *Or* the owner records this as an accepted pilot risk in §12.3 next to G1-13b.

**M10. MAJOR — §3.5 "Reads per tick", §10 test 35 — the statement bound cannot be measured as written.**
*Problem:* Test 35 counts SELECT statements on the connection with a spy and expects the same count for 1 and 150 due alerts. The same connection also runs `appendEvents` (1 SELECT per `append`, `append-events.ts:13`; fine) **and every projection's apply, per event** (`writer.ts:69`). The `alerts` projection has to compute `next_due_at` (it needs the alert's `values` for `window_ms` steps), update `alert_inbox.moved_to`, and replace `alert_recipients`. Done naturally, that is ≥ 1 read per `AttemptSent`, so 150 alerts → ≥ 150 reads.
*Failure:* The builder either can't get test 35 green, or narrows the spy until the test proves nothing.
*Fix:* Pick one: (a) carry `next_due_at` (and `moved_to` ids) in the `AttemptSent` / `EscalationStepped` / `StepSkipped` payloads, computed in `intents` from `batchReads`, so the projection is **write-only** (UPDATE / INSERT / DELETE by key; also makes rebuild independent of kind code changes); then the spy counts every SELECT on the connection and the bound holds. Or (b) scope the bound to `due` + `batchReads` and add a separate bound for projection statements per event (fixed, ≤ k). (a) is better.

**M11. MAJOR — §4.5 — approved text changed without a row.**
*Problem:* The same class of finding as G2 #2. CLAUDE.md says "never silently diverge".
- **00 M19 / §4.1 `processed_commands.payload_hash`**: the M4 change has no C-00 row.
- **Foundation §5.8 step 3**: §4.3 To 03a says it changes "through the v1.8 bump", but C-F has no row for it.
- **README R6** ("≈ 40,000 events per day"): §4.3 To 03a replaces it with ≈ 75k, but C-README has no row amending R6.
- **00 §6.33 Endpoint count**: H-5 adds §6.36–§6.38 but does not update the count. **00 §5 tables**: `app_releases` is a 00 table created in 01d's migration with no 00 §5 row.
- **00 §6.17 Q8 "last seen"**: alert-mode sockets go through `armKeepalive`, whose pong marks the device seen (`server.ts:259`). A session-less tablet now counts as "seen" in device health. That is likely right, but it is a 00 behaviour and needs a row (or H-1 must say alert mode does not call `deviceSeen`).
- **G0 §12 preamble** not marked "amended by 01d v0.2 #3" (`01d-g0-decisions.md:167`).
*Fix:* Add the rows with old → new text.

### MINOR

**m1. §3.9 + §3.5 day close — a double close in one tick.** At day close, `no_approver` is open with `expire_due = 1`, and the same projection pass sets the flag dirty. One tick writes `AlertClosed 'expired'` from `due`, then evaluates the flag from reads taken before that close, and may append `AlertClosed 'done'` for the same alert. *Fix:* the flag evaluation skips alerts closed earlier in the same pass. Test.

**m2. §3.7 — phone sessions.** The table does not say that `SessionStarted` / `SessionEnded` on a `phone` leave the owner row alone, or where the projection gets the device type during replay (from `DevicePaired.device_type`; `SessionStarted` has no type, `SessionStarted.v1.ts`). A naive build upserts the phone row with the session id, and the next logout deletes the owner binding. *Fix:* state both; add the phone logout case to test 27.

**m3. §6 batch 3 `receipt` — the "this device was sent" check has no source.** `alert_recipients` holds only the latest step, and per-socket memory dies on reconnect. A receipt for step 1 that arrives after step 2 is dropped as "not sent". *Fix:* check against the cumulative recipients (M2), and count `alerts_open` / catch-up as sent.

**m4. §3.7 alert mode — a cloned device token goes unnoticed.** Only the device token is needed. A token copied from a kiosk browser profile or an old tablet gives a laptop on staff Wi-Fi the lock texts and lets it post `delivered` receipts as the bound person. Both sockets live side by side. *Fix:* one alert-mode socket per device: a new one closes the old one (`4409`, logged with both remote addresses).

**m5. §3.6 receipt dedupe ignores the step.** The key `(alert, person, device, kind)` means escalation steps record no delivery once step 0 was delivered. §6.10 "per step" cannot show whether step 2 reached the manager's tablet. *Fix:* add `step` to the delivered key, or state that §6.10 shows the first delivery only.

**m6. §8.1–§8.2 — a crash between commit and send.** Frames committed before a hub crash are lost. Reconnect brings `alerts_open` / catch-up, but the spec does not say the client **re-notifies** an unseen urgent / action alert it gets there (Android notification plus sound once). Without that, a step lost to the crash is silent until the next step. *Fix:* state it; add a test with a lost frame and reconnect.

**m7. §6.1 raise step (4) — `ctx.permission.approver_role_ids`.** It is `null` when `raise` runs from a tick or a non-approvable command (`route.ts:249`, `:100`), so this crashes. It is also unused, since rule 6 reads approvers now. *Fix:* take it from `input.request.approver_role_ids`, or drop it from the payload.

**m8. §4.2 `dedupe_key` — one global namespace.** G1-21.4 says key = kind + subject + discriminator, but `raise` leaves that to the caller, and `alerts_open_dedupe` is on `dedupe_key` alone. Two kinds with the same key: the second gets `deduped: true` and the **other kind's** `alert_id`. A second requester whose raise is deduped cannot withdraw (`not_requester`). *Fix:* `raise` prefixes `kind + ':' + subject` itself (or index `(kind, dedupe_key)`), and states what a deduped requester sees.

**m9. §7 Performance — no test.** "raise ≤ 5 ms", "tick with 200 due ≤ 50 ms". better-sqlite3 blocks the event loop for the whole tick, so HTTP and WS wait too. *Fix:* a timed test on the pilot mini PC, in the style of 00 #79.

**m10. §10 test 23 — "crash before flush loses only that batch (simulated)" cannot fail.** *Fix:* test what restart must do: the dedupe set is seeded from `alert_receipts` of open alerts, and a receipt re-sent after the restart is recorded once.

**m11. §10 test 34 (a) needs a hook between `due` and `writer.run`.** Name it (`testHooks.betweenDueAndRun`, like `route.ts:92` `betweenGuardAndWriter`).

**m12. §5.3 — cloud volume not sized.** About 30k `audit` receipts and about 5k step events per day per branch go to cloud Postgres (D57 smallest tier). That is about 11M receipt rows per branch per year. *Fix:* state rows and bytes per day in §5.3 and carry the retention question to 06 / 07. No pilot breakage at one branch.

**m13. Pilot scope → §12.4.** (a) "Present today" (§3.11, `SetPresence`, `presence_today`, filter, tests 5 and 45): its own safety rule makes it do nothing unless someone ticks people every day, and no pilot flow needs it. Defer, unless M9's fix uses it. (b) `cashier_kiosk` and `phone` are accepted in alert mode, but §8 only says that the tablet service opens it. Either say when the kiosk and phone pages open alert mode, or drop those types.

**m14. §6.4 `MarkAlertsSeen` — a full command transaction per tap.** Each tap does the `processed_commands` insert with an fsync, even for non-request alerts that write no event. So A11's "no fsync per receipt" holds only for `delivered`. The load is fine (≈ 0.2 taps/s at rush). State it so nobody "optimises" it later.

**m15. §3.6 `alert_seen` is sent only on `RequestSeen`.** That is correct only while `urgent` ⇒ request (G0-2). *Fix:* `defineAlertKind` rejects `family: 'urgent'` without a request part (startup error).

## 3. Attack summary (what held)

- **Answer vs answer, answer vs close, answer vs withdraw, answer vs tick:** hold. There is one synchronous connection, `writer.run` has no `await`, the selection inside the transaction is authoritative, and tests 6 and 34 cover it. `checkPin` awaits argon2 **before** its `writer.run` (`pin.ts:441–451`, `:456`), so two PIN answers serialise correctly.
- **Raise vs raise:** holds (unique partial index plus the per-transaction map, test 47). Namespace issue: m8.
- **Answer after the right was removed:** holds. The directory is read inside the transaction (`route.ts:99–100`), and `StaffDirectoryApplied` bumps `authGeneration` (M39).
- **Replay or forged `AnswerRequest`:** holds. M19 replay needs the same device, type and payload (`route.ts:187–199`). A forged answer needs a live staff session. PIN exposure: M4.
- **Revoked device or rotated token mid-stream:** holds (H-1 after-commit close; rotation means re-pair, M60 / M40).
- **Restore:** bindings are exact by `session_id` with `hub_restored` (M40). Projections rebuild. A lost `processed_commands` row makes a re-sent answer return `409 "Already approved by <self>"`, which is harmless.
- **SQLite at rush:** 10 devices and 200 orders give < 1 write transaction per second from 01d (ticks only when due, receipts ≤ 1/s, approvals a few per hour). The write lock is not a risk. The risks are the event-loop pause (m9) and the unmeasurable bound (M10).
- **Contracts for 02 / 03a / 04 / 05 / 01e:** `registerTick`, `registerRoute`, `registerTargetResolver`, `close`, `byDedupeKey`, `recordReceipt` are enough. The gaps are M5, M6, M7, M8.

## 4. Suggested order for v0.4

M5, M6, M7, M8 first: they change §4.2 contracts that 02, 03a, 04 and 05 build on. Then M1, M2 and M10 (tick and projection correctness), M3 and M4 (cheating and PIN storage), M9 (binding: fix or owner-accepted risk), and M11 (rows). The MINORs can be fixed in one pass.
