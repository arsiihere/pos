## MAJOR

### M-1 — "Permission of the reversed command" cannot be built without an 00 change, and is the wrong key anyway
**Where:** S15 l. 92, S19 l. 96, §6.5 l. 339–348, test 19.
**Problem:** The pipeline resolves a command's permission statically from `CommandDef.permission` before the handler runs (`registry.ts:61`). A permission picked from the reversed event needs `permission: 'checked-in-handler'`, and that value is **allowed only for `HANDLER_CHECKED_COMMANDS = ['AnswerRequest', 'WithdrawRequest', 'MarkAlertsSeen']`** (00 v1.6 §3.7 H-3, `registry.ts:36`, enforced at registration `:109`). Using it means an **00 spec row** (missing from §4.5) and a handler that re-implements scope and the 05a device rules (listed-only, `everyone` downgrade). §6.5 also checks `404` before `403` (l. 344), against §3.2's order, so a cashier learns which event ids exist. On substance: letting the person who recorded a receipt also undo it is the very hole a daily sheet exists to close (fill and close by different people, S15's own reason).
**Fix (preferred, cut):** `ReverseInventoryEntry` takes the **fixed** permission `close-daily-sheet` (manager undoes; the store keeper asks). Static key, no 00 row, normal §3.2 order, test 19 simplified. Note: `close-daily-sheet` is listed-only, so an undo needs a listed device — state it. **Or** keep the rule and add 00 row "H-x: `ReverseInventoryEntry` joins `HANDLER_CHECKED_COMMANDS`; the handler resolves the key with scope + 05a §3.3 rules; `403` before `404`" with its tests.

### M-2 — A reversed entry cannot be re-entered on its own day, so a typo fix breaks two sheets
**Where:** S14 l. 91 (moves need the **open** day), S19 l. 96, §6.5 edges l. 348.
**Problem:** The owner's own case (O-3): 18 500 kg typed for 18.5 kg, noticed next morning. Yesterday is closed and has no sheet, so the reversal is accepted — but the **correct 18.5 kg cannot be recorded for yesterday** (`409 BUSINESS_DAY_NOT_OPEN`). It goes on today. Yesterday: closing count includes the 18.5 kg, `received` does not → false `under` 18.5 kg. Today: opening (= yesterday's closing) already holds it and `received` adds it again → false `over` 18.5 kg (≈ Rs 33 300 at Rs 1.80/g: far over the Rs 500 threshold). The MWA gets the right price on the wrong day's order. Two flags from one fixed typo.
**Fix:** `ReverseInventoryEntry` takes optional **`replacement`** (the corrected lines, same shape as the original command's lines, same S2–S4 / S19 checks); one transaction appends `InventoryEntryReversed` **and** the replacement event with the reversed event's `business_day_id` / `business_date` (allowed while that day has no sheet, the same condition). Tests 14 / 26 gain "reverse + replace on yesterday after today's open → yesterday's `received` = 18 500 g, today unaffected".

### M-3 — A sheet correction (`supersedes`) can correct nothing
**Where:** S14 l. 91 (counts only for "the open day or a closed day **without a sheet**"; correction "allowed while no later day has a sheet"), S19 (reversal only while "no sheet"), §6.7 edges l. 380, test 17, test 24 (`has_sheet` after the sheet).
**Problem:** Once the sheet exists, `RecordCount` and `ReverseInventoryEntry` for that day are refused `has_sheet`. A correction re-runs the close over **the same counts and moves**; the only things that can differ are `todaysCost` (now, not at the close — a silent re-pricing) and late 08c lines. The manager who finds a wrong count after closing has no way to fix it; the "correction" button just re-stamps the same numbers at a new cost.
**Fix:** While a day holds the **latest** sheet (no later day has a sheet), allow `RecordCount` and `ReverseInventoryEntry` (+ M-2 replacement) for it with `close-daily-sheet`; the day view shows "sheet out of date — re-close"; the correction then recomputes. The correction **re-uses** the superseded sheet's `unit_cost_micro` / `unit_cost_source` per item unless the owner wants re-pricing (state which). Test 17: count fixed after close → correction shows the new closing and the old cost.

### M-4 — The MWA recompute is a different fold from live apply and from rebuild
**Where:** S6 l. 83, S7 l. 84, S19 l. 96 ("recomputed by folding that item's remaining moves and counts in order", §6.5 "ordered by `day_seq, seq`"), §5.2 `inventory_counts` (latest count per day only), m-15 l. 240, tests 14, 22.
**Problem:** The MWA is **path-dependent**: each priced receipt weights by `on_hand` as known *at that moment*. (1) Live apply folds events by `seq`; the S19 recompute folds by `(day_seq, seq)`; a late count (next morning) sits at a later `seq` but an earlier `day_seq`, so the two orders give a different `on_hand` at the receipts in between → different `avg`. (2) The recompute reads `inventory_counts`, which keeps **only the latest** count per (day, item); live apply saw the superseded counts at their time. (3) A group rebuild (test 22) replays by `seq` — so live → reversal → rebuild gives a third `avg`. Test 14 ("exactly as without it") and test 22 ("reproduces exactly … reversed lines and late counts") cannot both hold. Later `unit_cost_micro` values frozen into waste and sheets then depend on whether and when a reversal or rebuild happened. `max_receipt_line` (S19 guard) is not said to be recomputed either, so a confirmed-then-reversed 18 500 kg keeps the guard dead.
**Fix:** One pure function `foldItemCost(events)` in `packages/domain/src/inventory/mwa.ts` over the item's **inventory-stream events in `seq` order** (reversed events skipped, S7's anchor evaluated at each priced line from the counts *seen so far*), returning `{ avg_micro, on_hand, max_receipt_line }`. Live apply = one step of it; the S19 recompute = the fold over the item's events read from the event store (the event ids via `inventory_moves` / a counts history read, 1 + 1 statements); rebuild = the same fold. Test: live, recompute-after-reversal and rebuild agree on every field for a history with a late count, a recount and a reversal. Row count of the recompute read stays "one item's history" (accepted, stated).

### M-5 — The tracked-cooked set is derived, so every batch that uses ghee becomes a mandatory nightly count, and the set is not frozen
**Where:** S2 l. 79 ("prepared stock items whose published batch recipe … uses ≥ 1 A-item"), S14 l. 91 (non-null count for **every** tracked cooked item), C-1 l. 170 (form hides `is_a_item` on prepared), m-8.
**Problem:** Ghee (an A-item) is in nearly every batch recipe: PAYE-COOKED, QEEMA-COOKED, the planned chanay, any tarka kept as a prepared item. By S2 each becomes a required count; the close refuses until someone types a number for every pot, kept overnight or not. Staff will type 0 for what is there → actual overstated → false `over`, the failure M-1 of run 1 was about. R39 (b) "5 A-items, one screen" grows with each recipe the owner publishes, without the owner choosing it. The set is also computed from the snapshot **at close**: a recipe published or an A-item ticked overnight adds a required count for last night that nobody can physically take next morning.
**Fix:** Make the set **explicit**: a prepared item is tracked when the owner ticks it. Re-use `is_a_item` on `prepared` items (08a already allows it) as "count on the daily sheet" — S2 becomes "A-items = `is_a_item` ∧ kind ∈ ingredient, packet; tracked cooked = `is_a_item` ∧ kind = prepared ∧ its explosion uses ≥ 1 A-item" — and drop C-1's "hide on prepared" (rename the form label "Count daily"). Freeze the required set at the day's **first count** (or at `BusinessDayClosed`), store it in `DailySheetClosed`, and require counts only for items in it. Owner question: confirm PAYE-COOKED is the only tracked cooked item for the pilot.

### M-8 — `theoreticalUsage` cannot say "this item's usage is incomplete", and the period rules are missing
**Where:** S12 l. 89, §4.1 l. 135, R-c l. 153, S13 `coverage` reason l. 90.
**Problem:** (1) 08b `explodeUsage` drops an item whose EP line has no yield and marks the **line** `partial` (08b B11). Beef in "tawa piece" is an EP line; if beef's yield is archived, beef's theoretical is under-stated on every sale while **coverage by revenue** (recipes present) stays 100 % → beef `over` every night, never `unreliable`. The return shape has no per-item partial. (2) A period of several days (S13 `chain_broken`) calls the function per day and "sums" — `coverage_bp` cannot be summed and no rule is given. (3) Voided lines **without** a disposition (voided before preparation) are not named (excluded, presumably). (4) A sale line that reaches the hub after the day's sheet (offline replay) changes that day's theoretical after the sheet is frozen; nothing tells the manager.
**Fix:** R-c return per item `{ sold, comp, void_waste, staff_meal, partial: boolean }`; S13 adds reason `usage_partial`. Period coverage = revenue-weighted: R-c returns `{ coverage_bp, revenue_minor }` and 08d combines `Σ(coverage × revenue) ÷ Σ revenue` with `divRound`. R-c: "voids without disposition are excluded". (4): accept and state in §7 risks (the correction of M-3 picks them up).

### M-10 — Sales after the count give a false `under` every night that hides real loss
**Where:** §7 risk (4) l. 402, OQ-9 l. 570 ("No", default by the author, not an owner ruling).
**Problem:** A paye house sells until late. Counted at 22:00, sold until 23:30: those plates are in the day's theoretical (S12, by placing day) but were still on the shelf at the count, so closing is high and actual low → `under` by that night's late sales. It is systematic, so it cancels a real nightly loss of the same size; with a 3 % AND Rs 500 rule, the flag the sheet exists for rarely fires. **Fix (cheap, owner to pick):** (a) at close, **1 read** of the day's last order-line `seq` (or 08c returns it with `theoreticalUsage`); any item whose closing count `seq` is before it gets reason `sales_after_count` (so the flag says "recount after service", never hides silently); or (b) the day view's Count action is enabled only after the day's last open order is billed (screen rule, no read at close). Put it to the owner as OQ-9 again; do not default it.
